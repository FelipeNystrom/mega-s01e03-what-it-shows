Parser: day-first input interpreted month-first

The shared parser reads month before day. For a day-first tenant, app/email paths and booking-v2 form input can therefore swap the intended month and day. Harbour Dental is en-GB, booking-v2: R-41200 describes 7 April becoming 4 July. Morrow Tax is fr-FR, booking-v2: R-41205 describes 3 October becoming 10 March even though its browser was en-US and intake called it hint. Tenant locale—not browser locale—is the relevant setting. Correct the parser’s interpretation of the tenant’s input; changing booking-v3’s hint does not repair this path.

Hint: booking-v3 instructs the wrong input order

For a day-first tenant, booking-v3 displays MM/DD/YYYY while the form reads the input in the tenant’s day-first order. Following that instruction causes a different swap. Copper Studio is es-ES, booking-v3: form report R-41230 describes 6 January becoming 1 June but was tagged parser. Alder Physio is de-DE, booking-v3: R-41241 is another form-path example, with an en-US browser. Fix the displayed instruction to agree with the tenant-aware form interpretation. Do not change this form to parse month-first merely to make the incorrect hint true.

The existing classifier uses tenant locale and form plus report channel: day-first booking-v3 form reports are hint; other supported day-first paths are parser. A booking-v3 tenant’s app report is not automatically a hint report. Intake tags are initial guesses and must not be used to select either engineering fix.

Receipt time is not merge time

The received series records when the desk first had each distinct report. The merged series records when the desk attached it to this incident. The later bulk merge in July is queue administration, not the onset of the product failures. For example, R-41200 arrived on 1 April and was merged on 2 July; R-41230 arrived on 17 April and was also merged on 2 July. Use the table’s receipt ranges to describe each cause’s observed history. Neither receipt dates nor a merge spike establish deployment dates or the date the underlying bug was introduced.

What the one-day notes do—and do not—show

The table also renders the desk’s one-day-move notes under their tenant-classified cause. For example, R-41202 describes 11 May versus 12 May rather than an obvious month/day swap. Preserve and investigate those descriptions; the existing classifier still files them under parser. A desk note is not independent evidence of a third, timezone-related product defect. Do not silently discard these reports or claim the parser reproduction explains every detail of their bodies.

Repair the dedupe decision, not its history

The accepted triage page follows decision 0001: normalize a subject and merge without reading the body. The executable examples already refute that policy:

  • Alder Physio form versus app can share “Booking on the wrong date” but have different causes: hint versus parser.
  • Kettle Books app versus email can have different subjects but the same parser cause.
  • Copper Studio and Morrow Tax form reports can share “Date is wrong” while using different booking forms and therefore different causes.
  • unknown.sender cannot be assigned a cause from the tenant directory, even when both subjects are identical.

This is a systemic desk merge error, not an additional date-product bug. For this incident, dedupe exported rows by ID first, resolve the tenant, read the report and channel, and compare the supported cause before merging. Subjects may help search; they cannot authorize a merge. Group proven same-cause reports into their respective issues, retain original IDs and receipt timestamps, and preserve a trace of the old merge. Unknown tenants, unsupported locale/path combinations and contradictory bodies need manual triage rather than a guess or an automatic merge.

Leave the accepted ADR unchanged as a historical record of what the desk decided. Record a superseding decision before treating the safer rule as accepted policy; this signed review documents the contradiction and proposed correction, not a retroactive rewrite of the old acceptance.
